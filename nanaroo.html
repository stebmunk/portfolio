Overview

The problem

Nanaroo is a client project. The client’s mission is to reduce the daily stress and burden of dementia caregivers as they care for their loved ones. Care is rarely a one-person job, so the app is designed as a team-based collaboration tool: family, friends, and professional caregivers share one picture of the loved one’s care.

The work below follows each phase of the design process, from research and flow mapping through wireframes, early UI iterations, the design system, and the MVP screens.

01 · Research

Workflow, research & competitive analysis

I mapped several core UX flows and delivered research on competitors and caregiving behavior.

02 · Flows & wireframes

Conceptual wireframes

Conceptual wireframes for the care clients list and caregiver shift views, including the care-entry input and AI processing flow.

Design notes from the wireframe board
  • Both the care client and the caregiver are displayed at the top of the view.
  • The current caregiver on shift is displayed on the right, visible only to the care manager/director.
  • Care items for the day are a checklist of sorts. They are the only card-styled items, intentionally, to isolate them and put focus on them for caregivers.
  • A full-width + button makes the primary activity trigger very clear to users.
  • Each processed care entry captures caregiver name, shift info, client/loved one, submission date and time, the user entry, location, detected care tags, other users’ names, a summary/title, urgency level (if indicated), and the loved one’s mood/status.

05 · MVP

MVP screens

Screens from the MVP build, shown by feature. Click any screen to open it full size.

“Loved-ones” Home

Manage care for multiple loved ones in one place. Family, friends, and professional caregivers can create care teams or be invited to join them.

Care

Caregivers log completed care here. AI processing turns long, multi-event notes into complete, standardized, categorized entries, so users stay fast and accurate without juggling multiple input fields.

Care Items / Filters

After AI processing finishes (about 10–15 seconds), entries appear here as a detailed record of a shift’s or a day’s caregiving.

Alerts

Must-see information lives in Alerts. A new alert takes over the screen the next time each caregiver opens the app, and view tracking shows the whole team who has seen it.

Chat

Team chat keeps caregivers in sync, with support for text and images.

Team

Community is key to caregiving, so adding team members is a simple flow. Add or remove members as needed for each care team.

Loved One Profile

The loved one is at the heart of the app. This profile holds the key information about the person receiving care.

User Profile

Your profile is where you adjust notification settings, update personal information, and find important app information.

06 · Status

Status & what I learned

Status: Live on the App Store and Google Play.

What I learned:

  • Design for stressed, time-poor caregivers. Caregivers often use the app in the middle of a hard day, so every core action needs to be fast, obvious, and forgiving, with one clear primary button instead of many input fields.
  • Make AI-processed entries trustworthy. When AI turns a quick note into a structured care record, people need to see what it captured and how it was categorized so the whole care team can rely on it.
